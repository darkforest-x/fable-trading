# Audit who enters and leaves the high-score cohort before blaming a feature

- **Problem**: ETH's four BTC-context inputs improved the unchanged near-target admission policy from +7.61R to +15.40R, while the within-quarter top decile fell from +22.27R to -1.34R. Training gain alone could not explain the disagreement.
- **Dead end**: More total profit does not establish better tail ranking; a negative tail does not imply every newly promoted event lost. Removing the directional pair also did not repair the tail: state-only remained -1.69R. Calling excluded events "penalized" would additionally confuse relative rank with absolute score.
- **Effective path**: Freeze each quarter's cohort size and partition the old/new tails into shared, promoted, and displaced events. The four-input model replaced 18 events worth +26.72R with 18 worth +3.10R; the shared 18 were unchanged. Explain all test events with paired training-only backgrounds, checking exact score reconstruction. Promoted events' mean score rose by 1.36R; correlation/beta contributions were about 0.99-1.01R across references. Displaced events' scores also rose by 0.073R on average, yet their relative ranks fell. Attribution describes the fitted models, not causal market effects or the effect of deleting a feature without refitting.
- **General rule**: First reconcile the cohort membership and realized-return identity, then investigate score changes. Preserve failed outcomes and matched random controls. Do not use retrospective top-decile membership as an executable gate or infer that context inputs have no value merely because their highest-ranked cohort underperforms.
