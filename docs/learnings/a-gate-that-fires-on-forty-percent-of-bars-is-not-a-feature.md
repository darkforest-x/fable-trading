# A gate that fires on 40% of bars is not a feature; measure its base rate before scanning

- **Problem**: The owner's ETH 1m example (2026-10-08 23:02 break) had to be turned into a scannable rule. The obvious move was to reuse the SPIKE V13.1 dense-rope gate as the "dense" condition: mean six-MA width over the prior 12 bars ≤ 3 ATR and ≥ 2 flips.
- **Dead end**: That gate is true on 41.8% of ETH 1m bars. Breakouts that pass it (`knot_v131`, 128k–493k events per timeframe) beat matched random entries by about 0 on every timeframe from 1m to 1H (5R shorts: −0.06 to +0.07R). The one cell that met the pre-registered pass bar was this loose gate on 1H shorts (p 0.45/0.036), which is about what chance gives across 200 cells. A condition that holds on almost half the bars only re-labels "any breakout"; it cannot be what made the example special.
- **What worked**: First measure where the example's own values sit in the distribution. Its width of 0.56 ATR is at the 0.66th percentile. Requiring both "≤ 0.7 ATR and ≥ 10 flips" holds on only 0.5% of bars. That tight knot (`primary`) gives positive short-side excess over random in both periods on 1m–30m: +0.02 to +0.26R at 5R, versus about 0 for the loose gate. So the tightness is the feature, not "dense" in general. The edge is still smaller than cost. With the stop beyond the six MAs, the 1m median stop is 0.10% of price, so 0.2% cost is 2.4R per trade and net is about −2.2R. The best family, 15m shorts with confirmation, has gross +0.26/+0.19R and net −0.12/−0.04R.
- **General rule**: (1) Before adding a gate to a rule, compute its base rate: what share of all bars it holds on. A gate above roughly 10% is almost certainly not the shape the owner pointed at. (2) Read whether the edge beats random entries and whether it covers cost separately. A positive excess with negative net means the signal is real but too small for the stop size ([relative-pattern-edge-can-still-fail-the-cost-floor](relative-pattern-edge-can-still-fail-the-cost-floor.md), [exact-stop-distance-turns-fixed-cost-into-variable-r-tax](exact-stop-distance-turns-fixed-cost-into-variable-r-tax.md)). (3) On low timeframes, compute cost / median stop first. Above about 1R, no signal quality can rescue the rule, so do not scan lower first.

Changing those constants is an owner threshold decision; this note does not change them.
