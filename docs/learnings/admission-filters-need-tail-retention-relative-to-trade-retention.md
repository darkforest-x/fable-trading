# Compare winner retention with trade retention before claiming a useful filter

- **Problem**: ETH range-position and4H context masks reduced total losses but often removed most10R/20R winners. The owner wanted selective rejection of ineffective entries while keeping large moves.
- **Dead end**: Comparing cumulative R alone rewards taking fewer trades in a losing pool. Strict intersections can look safer by deleting winners too. Relaxing to a union can preserve80% of winners simply by retaining80% of all trades. An unconditional random comparator also conflates context selection with the entry signal if its own context is ignored.
- **Effective path**: Reuse the frozen independent native ledger, apply admission masks only to causal entry fields, and report mean net basis points, winner retention, trade retention and watch-event counts together. Apply the identical predicate to each control at its own time, then rematch within week/side/volatility/segment without replacement; report incomplete matching coverage. Keep the original2024 selection frozen when assessing2025/2026. Separate the later exploratory OR masks from the original15-arm design and preserve both sets of outputs.
- **Evidence**: In `exp-eth-range-context-20261020-v1`, common observable baseline4068trades averages-17.640bp.4H-arrow alignment retains2478trades,33/53gross20R winners and averages-17.733bp. Monday-edge OR4H-momentum retains3229trades(79.38%),42/53gross20R winners(79.25%) and averages-17.576bp; later mean worsens versus baseline. All22filter arms remain net negative over the full period. This rejects these filters as an upgrade, not all possible range or higher-timeframe information.
- **General rule**: Require admission filters to improve net expectancy and preserve disproportionately useful events; report raw count reductions separately. A historically promising component can fail later. No discarded signal may change the retained trades' independent stop/exit paths. Known-history exploratory additions never become untouched validation.
