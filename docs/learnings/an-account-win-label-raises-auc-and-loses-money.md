# An account win label raises AUC and loses money

- **Problem**: The 1m entry score lost to the frozen pre-touch score on the paper accounts. Two explanations were open. Its fixed 0.5/0.6 cuts admitted more trades than the frozen score did, and it was trained on a generic rebound label instead of the accounts' own exits. `exp-monday-limit-1m-account-label-20261031-v1` tested both as separate arms: rank-mapped cuts, then a label of net > 0 under the v3 and spike account rules. Training data ended before 2026, and 2026 was the test year.
- **Dead end**: The account-label model looked best by AUC: 0.81 on the 2026 v3 win label, against 0.68 for the old 1m score and 0.63 for the frozen score. It did worst with money. Its top decile averaged +11bp, against +30bp for the old 1m score. 2026 v3 per-trade dropped from 81bp with the frozen score to 25bp, and the allin3 runs that reached x10 fell from 96 percent to 5 percent. The 5m-only control scored AUC 0.80 with a top decile of -40bp. The five shuffled-1m models matched it at 0.797-0.798. So most of that AUC comes from 5m fields such as side and range bucket, which predict whether a trade wins, not how much it makes. The label net > 0 counts a small win the same as a full opposite-1.618 target.
- **Effective path**: Matching the trade count by rank was the fair change. It restored the 1m score's per-trade mean (v3 101bp vs 81bp; allin3 235bp vs 125bp in 2026). It still did not beat the frozen score's account multiples (v3add20 x0.95 vs x1.33, spike x0.86 vs x1.19). Against its own shuffled null, the 1m model is better by about 70-100bp per trade. The 1m features do carry information, but not enough to replace the frozen score.
- **General rule**: For a resting-limit strategy with variable payoff, never select a model by AUC on a binary win label. Compare arms by account replay at a matched trade count, and include a feature-shuffled null fitted the same way. If a label is needed, weight it by payoff, for example net R. Plain win/loss is not enough.
