# Reduced exposure is not better trade selection

- **Problem**: V13's known-opportunity mean improved after a higher-timeframe
  filter, while actual retained trades lost more per trade. The apparent
  improvement could be mistaken for trend-selection skill.
- **Dead end**: Counting95 avoided losses against32 missed winners ignores their
  unequal amounts; subtracting the original251 mean from248 known opportunities
  also changes the denominator. Neither is an entry-quality estimate.
- **Effective path**: Keep actual trades and all-opportunity returns separate.
  The121 retained trades average-18.954bp, the127 rejected ones originally
  -9.479bp. On the same248 known pairs D+4.854bp includes abstention0, while
  fixed-control I-1.653bp shows controls benefited more. Three unknowns remain
  unpriced. This is less exposure, not demonstrated profitable selection.
- **General rule**: An admission gate must improve executable economics and its
  fixed counterfactual, not merely reduce the number of losing exposures. Report
  the value of foregone winners as well as the number of avoided losses.
- **Affected**: V13 report/three-state ledgers; full251 target and154fixed triples
  unchanged. Reused development is exploratory, not independent confirmation.
