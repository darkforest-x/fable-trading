# Importing a factor requires its numerical contract, not just its name

- **Problem**: The workbench needs reusable open-source factors for MA-density research. A familiar name does not establish the formula actually implemented by a pinned upstream version.
- **Dead end**: Treating VSTD as standard deviation divided by mean volume would silently import a different factor: Qlib Alpha158 divides by current volume. Copying upstream rolling defaults also emits partial-window estimates that appear fully warmed up. These alternatives were rejected during source inspection, before economic experiments.
- **Effective path**: Pin source commits and licenses, store expression/input/window/adaptation metadata beside executable code, and independently check formulas. The adapter keeps Qlib ddof=1 and correlation constant guards, explicitly requires full windows, and retains undefined values. CHOP14 requires 15 raw bars locally so every TR has its prior close; that stricter boundary is recorded rather than presented as exact upstream startup parity.
- **General rule**: Before importing a named indicator, inspect its denominator, warmup, zero handling, and availability time. Appending or perturbing future bars must leave past results unchanged. Formula correctness and causal computation do not establish economic value.
- **Scope**: `yoyo/research_workspace/open_factors.py`, `open_factors.json`, license notices, catalog and factor UI. Existing L2 feature order, strategy parameters, costs, and training/production eligibility remain independent contracts. These 14 candidates need separate single-factor, time-separated economic tests with the original matched controls.
