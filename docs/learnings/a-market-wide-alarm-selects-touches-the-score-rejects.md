# A market-wide spike alarm selects 1.618 touches that work, and the entry score rejects almost all of them

- **Problem**: The owner proposed this rule: when the market-wide spike alarm fires (≥ 25 coins touching the same-side Monday 1.618 within 5 minutes), enter every later touch at once, then at the touch bar's close exit if the real score is below the cut (`exp-monday-limit-alarm-entry-20261029-v1`, NautilusTrader, 129 alarms 2021–2026).
- **Dead ends**:
  - The close-time score check. In alarm windows, 97% of touches score below 0.5 at the touch close. The alarm conditions push the frozen features (24h move toward the level, volume) to values the model reads as bad. Exiting at the close also books the spike while price is through the level: −25bp on those touches against +70bp for holding them. The rule turned 3.70x into 1.41x.
  - Expecting the per-touch score to carry the information. Outside alarms, the same enter-all rule loses (−6bp, 0.33x, drawdown 81%). The alarm is the selector, not the score.
- **What worked**: Enter every later same-side touch within 30 minutes of an alarm at the level and hold (near edge, −30%): +80bp over 2146 trades, 3.70x with 17% drawdown since 2021, and every period positive, including 2025 (1.31x), the year every score-based variant lost. Across 96 episodes the mean was +162bp and 65% were positive. Longer windows (2h, 6h) were weaker but still positive.
- **General rule**: Test the regime selector on its own before attaching a per-trade filter to it. A model trained on ordinary conditions can systematically reject the extreme regime where the edge lives. Count independent episodes, not trades: about 100 here.
- **Caveats**: The 25-coin threshold was picked on the same history (2026-10-27), and the result is mostly longs (122 of 129 alarms). Live trading needs limit or trigger orders on all untouched same-side levels within seconds of the alarm. No random-time control inside alarm windows was run, so "buy anything after a crash alarm" is not ruled out.
