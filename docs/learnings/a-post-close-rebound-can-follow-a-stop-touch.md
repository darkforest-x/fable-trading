# A post-close rebound can follow a stop touch

- **问题**：The PROVE example showed a large rebound after a completed 5m candle, but a usable entry also needs a contemporaneous score, a target and an explicit stop hypothesis.
- **死胡同**：The initial explanation compared the entry close with later prices and concentrated on reclaim timing. That comparison omits the intervening path through a proposed stop and mixes the spike near-edge target with a signal that only qualifies for the existing v3 score gate. No new trading simulation was run.
- **有效路径**：The persisted score is 0.5677789766 and EMA order is 1: eligible for the current v3 score >=0.5 pool, ineligible for spike score >=0.6/order >=3. V3's 0.5–0.6 target is 0.178 + 0.618*(0.1889-0.178) = 0.1847362. The 12:00 Beijing touch-bar close was 0.1684 and that candle's low was 0.1674. The following candle reached 0.1656, crossing a hypothetical stop at 0.1674 before the subsequent rebound. This establishes a stop-price touch, not a modeled fill or net PnL. At the 12:10 reclaim close 0.1746, the known event low 0.1656 gives 5.1546% risk and 5.8054% target space, only 1.1262 gross reward/risk. Using the confirming candle's low 0.1683 instead gives 3.6082% risk and 1.6089 gross reward/risk. Neither stop is the approved v3 backstop; both remain separate research hypotheses.
- **通用规则**：For a close-entry example, join score identity, score time and strategy eligibility before selecting the target. Then inspect the path after the decision and calculate risk from the actual entry. A later high cannot establish that a stop-based strategy captured the rebound. A frozen score for the original entry/exit problem is not a calibrated success probability for a new close-entry/wick-stop problem.
