# Scoring at the instant of the touch selects worse than scoring at the bar before: the edge arrives after the touch

- **Problem**: The frozen Monday1618 score looked strongest when it read the whole touch bar, which is lookahead. The remaining hope was to score at the millisecond the price first trades through the level (Binance aggTrades), then enter at market after 100 / 300 / 1000 ms (`exp-monday-limit-tick-entry-20261029-v1`, last five months, full account rules in NautilusTrader).
- **Dead ends**:
  - Expecting the instant score to sit between the bar-before score and the leaky touch-bar score. At the instant the price is at the level, so toward24 (24h move toward the level) is at its maximum. Its coefficient is negative, so the score falls. Only the volume of the bar so far is visible, not the rebound. Instant ≥ 0.5 passed 326 touches, against 435 for the bar-before score and 450 leaky. With no latency at all, the selected v3 trades earned +128bp, against +205bp for resting on the bar-before score.
  - Blaming latency. Median slippage against the level was only about 4bp at 100–1000 ms (p90 44–57bp), plus the taker fee. That costs about 45bp per trade on v3, but selection loses more.
- **What worked**: Locate the touch in the trades and check parity first (touch-bar high 100%, low 99.95%, trade quantity against the 5m volume median 1.00). Then compare four variants on identical framework exits: resting, instant at the level, instant at market, and resting plus instant. The seconds layer added about 5 trades and lowered every account. v3add20 went 2.76x → 2.68x; spike instant-only lost.
- **General rule**: When a feature's predictive power came from data after the decision, moving the decision closer to the event does not recover it if the value at the decision instant is biased in the wrong direction. Check what each feature equals at the decision moment, not just whether it is available then.
