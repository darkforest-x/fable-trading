# A common-shock warning does not identify a profitable rebound entry

- **Problem**: After the August22 FHE wick, the owner asked whether earlier declines in other coins could provide time to prepare limits in lagging coins.
- **Dead end**: The 92 cached touch-day minute files show deterioration before FHE's touch, but their availability was selected by later historical touches. Treating their breadth as an ex-ante full-market alarm would make the selection itself look predictive. Prior static book-depth, OI/funding and24h breadth studies also failed; their existence is not evidence that adding the same fields will fix this problem.
- **Effective path**: Timestamp each completed minute at open+1minute. At13:08, the available13:07 candle shows XRP-2.117%, DOGE-1.533%, BTC-0.317%;88of92cached symbols declined. FHE did not touch the referenced level until13:11:33.608. This demonstrates nominal time separation in one event, not a stable leader/follower rule or profitable fill. Use the six existing complete major-coin histories to test an alarm over ordinary times as well as crashes; only then test lagging-coin selection and execution separately.
- **General rule**: Separate three targets: a common shock, a target contract's subsequent deep move, and recovery after that move. A thin book can raise fill probability while worsening adverse selection. Label non-rebounding crashes and false alarms explicitly, freeze universes from information available at the time, and keep all coins from one crash in one evaluation block. Report lead time after measured data/order delays; a sampled liquidation stream is a confirmation source, not a complete count or necessarily an early predictor.
