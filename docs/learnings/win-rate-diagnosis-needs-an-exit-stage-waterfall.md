# Diagnose low win rate separately from the cause of negative expectancy

- **Problem**: ETH 15m-watch/5m-entry produced 9.56% net winners. A large fee burden and visible high-R excursions made it tempting to explain that win rate only with fees or to assume missing winners.
- **Dead end**: Net R, net win rate and peak R describe different outcomes. A fee can erase substantial total expectancy while converting relatively few winning trades into losses. An armed trailing flag also does not establish profitable protection.
- **Effective path**: Reuse the 3,838-row frozen signal ledger and partition by exit stage. There were 3,288 initial stops before arming, 548 armed trades and two terminal boundary exits. Of the armed trades, 149 ended gross negative. Gross winners numbered 401; costs converted 34 into losses, leaving 367 net winners. Thus pre-arming failures and unprotected giveback explain most of the low win rate, while median cost of about 1R explains much of the negative net expectancy. Matching random controls showed 10.87% gross / 10.21% net wins under the same policy.
- **General rule**: First reconcile an exhaustive exit-stage count, gross-to-net winner conversion, and cost expressed in original risk units. Distinguish observed favorable excursion, close-trigger activation, actual stop protection and realized payout before changing entry or exit rules. A terminal-bar OHLC ambiguity must not be converted into an executable peak-profit claim.
