# Flash-crash attribution and execution require synchronized clocks

- **Problem**: FHE's 2026-08-22 Shanghai 13:10–13:15 candle suggested that a resting order was necessary to catch a fast rebound. The question also asked whether other coins shared the event and what caused it.
- **Dead end**: A single chart cannot distinguish a market-wide shock from a contract-specific move, and a news story about aggregate liquidations cannot identify the initiating FHE order. Reading complete overlapping histories for a one-day observation also caused unnecessary external-disk contention; a bounded date read was checked against a full FHE parse before use.
- **Effective path**: Reuse the cached Binance 1m bars and aggTrades. FHE first traded through the displayed 1.618 level at 13:11:33.608, bottomed at 13:12:09.114, and first regained the displayed target at 13:13:48.300. Compare other contracts in the identical 13:10–13:15 interval, and identify their low minute before claiming subsequent recovery. BASED, ACT, BSB, CHILLGUY and HMSTR also bottomed at 13:11 or 13:12. Check the aggregate trade volume/high/low against the 5m candle; treat the event as observed prices, not simulated fills. The stored FHE close score was 0.657627 while the prior-bar score was 0.531486, so the close score cannot authorize the earlier hypothetical order.
- **General rule**: Align venue, instrument, timezone, interval, and information availability before comparing flash moves. Separate the observed common shock, the unverified initiating cause, and executable order economics. A recovered low neither guarantees a resting fill nor identifies which adverse paths were knowable beforehand. Report prices as prices, not account profits.
- **Coverage boundary**: All 638 archived series had a timestamp for the event, but 125 of the 632 classified crypto series had zero volume. Count the 507 traded contracts for active-market breadth, not every stored timestamp. Historical archives with stale bars are not a point-in-time instrument listing.
