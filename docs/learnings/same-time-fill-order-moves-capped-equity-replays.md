# Under a gross cap, the order of same-time fills moves the compounded result

- **Problem**: Three equity replays of the same trades gave the last-five-months current rule as 1.36x, 1.44x and 1.48x. The trades were the Monday 1.618 limit-reclaim v2 fills: top-40 score, 10 percent per trade, gross capped at 1x. The fills, costs and sizing were identical in all three replays.
- **Dead ends**: First suspicion was a data difference. The trade counts and the per-trade mean matched exactly (3,724 trades, +22.5bp). The cause was the row order at identical entry timestamps. Many alts fill in the same 5m bar during market-wide spikes; cluster24 averages 30. Once 10 of them are open, the 1x cap is full. Every later row in that bar gets no capital. One script used pandas' unstable `sort_values`, one kept concatenation order, and one kept a merge order. Each therefore funded a different subset of the cluster.
- **What worked**: Sort deterministically by `(entry, event)` with a stable sort. Repeat the whole procedure (parameter selection and test) under 20 random orders of same-time fills, then report the median, the 10–90 percent range and the share of orders in which a candidate beats the baseline. The score-weighted sizing result survived this check: better in 20/20 orders in 2026, 17/20 in the last five months.
- **General rule**: When a capped equity replay compares sizing rules, treat the tie order as a source of noise. Make the sort stable and repeat the replay under random tie orders before calling a difference of about 0.1x real. Never compare multiples from two replays whose tie orders differ.
